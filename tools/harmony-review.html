counting in semitones

The same seven pitches sit in both spaces below. On the staff they carry register and spelling. On the clock they carry distance. Hover a pitch in either space to find it in the other, and click any pitch on the clock to make it scale degree 1.

musical space

The clef fixes identity and register: it tells you that this line is E and that space is F. It says nothing about the distance between them. That comes from the scale, which runs two, two, one, two, two, two, one. Rhythm is set aside here, so every pitch is a whole note.

pitch-class space

Twelve positions, each a semitone from its neighbor, numbered from C at 0. One position is a semitone and two positions are a whole tone. Register and spelling fall away: every C is 0, and C♯ and D♭ are both 1.

Reading a distance on the staff means counting letter names and then correcting for where the semitones fall. Reading it on the clock means subtracting. Degrees 3 to 4 and 7 to 8 are 1. Every other adjacent pair in the scale is 2. The key signature changes with the tonic. The pattern does not.
