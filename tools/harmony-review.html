mus 312 · harmony review · part one

counting in semitones

The same seven pitches sit in both spaces below. On the staff they carry register and spelling. On the clock they carry distance. Hover a pitch in either space to find it in the other.

musical space

The clef fixes identity and register: it tells you that this line is E and that space is F. It says nothing about the distance between them. That comes from the scale, which runs two, two, one, two, two, two, one. In C the semitones land at E to F and B to C. Rhythm is set aside here, so every pitch is a whole note.

pitch-class space

Twelve positions, each a semitone from its neighbor, numbered from C at 0. One position is a semitone and two positions are a whole tone. Register and spelling fall away: every C is 0, and C♯ and D♭ are both 1.

Reading a distance on the staff means counting letter names and then correcting for where the semitones fall. Reading it on the clock means subtracting. E to F and B to C are 1. Every other adjacent pair in the scale is 2.
