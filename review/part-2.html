Outside the four: minor v, which lacks a leading tone and harmonizes ♭7̂ in a bass descending from the tonic; and ♭VII, which usually acts as the dominant of ♭III and is labelled V/♭III when it does.

Caplin counts three functions. vi goes with the tonic, substituting for I in a deceptive resolution, and iii is an occasional dominant substitute in progressions such as iii6 to I.

3, the cadential six-four

the cadential six-four

Write the cadential six-four and its resolution in C major and in C minor, with figures. Say what the sixth and the fourth do and what the harmony is across both chords. Give the label in this course's notation and in Caplin's.

C major: bass G held, C and E above it, moving to B and D. C minor: bass G held, C and E♭ above it, moving to B♮ and D. Figures 6/4 to 5/3.

The sixth and the fourth fall by step to the fifth and the third. The harmony is dominant throughout: only after both have fallen is the dominant triad sounding.

This course: Cad6/4 to V. Caplin: one harmony, V(6-4 5-3).

  1. I – IV – vii° – iii – vi – ii – V
  2. I – V – vi – iii – IV – I
  3. I6 – vii°6 – vi6 – V6 – IV6 – iii6
  4. I – vi6 – ii – vii°6 – iii – I6 – IV – ii6
  5. I – V – ii – vi – iii
  6. I – iii – V – vii°

In the descending fifths the vii° stands in root position with its root doubled, passing rather than acting as a dominant. The descending seconds run entirely in first inversion. The ascending seconds come from the 5–6 motion over each bass note.

Inside a sequence the syntax is suspended: the V after I in the descending thirds is a link in the pattern, not a cadence.
