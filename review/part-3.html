chromatic harmony

Applied dominants, applied leading-tone chords, the augmented sixths, the Neapolitan, and modal mixture. Applied chords take the slash form with a curved arrow to the target.

1, applied dominants

applied dominant chords

Spell each applied dominant as a triad, add its seventh, and name the chord it points to.

  1. D major: V/ii, V/iii, V/IV, V/V, V/vi
  2. C minor: V/♭III, V/iv, V/V, V/♭VI
  3. Say which diatonic triads of a major key cannot be tonicized.

D major. V/ii: B D♯ F♯, seventh A, to E minor. V/iii: C♯ E♯ G♯, seventh B, to F♯ minor. V/IV: D F♯ A, seventh C, to G major. V/V: E G♯ B, seventh D, to A major. V/vi: F♯ A♯ C♯, seventh E, to B minor.

C minor. V/♭III: B♭ D F, seventh A♭, to E♭ major. V/iv: C E♮ G, seventh B♭, to F minor. V/V: D F♯ A, seventh C, to G major. V/♭VI: E♭ G B♭, seventh D♭, to A♭ major.

Only a major or minor triad is tonicized. The tonic already has its dominant in the key, and the triad on 7̂ is diminished. In minor, ♭VII most often acts as the dominant of ♭III rather than as a target.

2, the accidentals an applied dominant takes

applied dominant chords

In C major, name the diatonic triad each applied dominant stands on and the accidental or accidentals that turn it major.

  1. V/ii
  2. V/iii
  3. V/IV
  4. V/V
  5. V/vi
  1. Stands on vi, a minor triad. The third rises: A C♯ E.
  2. Stands on vii°, a diminished triad. The third and the fifth rise: B D♯ F♯.
  3. Stands on I, already major, and takes no accidental: C E G. The seventh B♭ is what marks it.
  4. Stands on ii, a minor triad. The third rises: D F♯ A.
  5. Stands on iii, a minor triad. The third rises: E G♯ B.

3, applied leading-tone chords

applied leading-tone chords

In C major, spell the fully diminished seventh applied to each of ii, iii, IV, V, and vi. Say which of the five targets also admits a half-diminished seventh and spell those. Then write the resolution of vii°7/V to V in four voices, naming what each sensitive tone does.

Roots sit a semitone below the root of the target. vii°7/ii: C♯ E G B♭. vii°7/iii: D♯ F♯ A C. vii°7/IV: E G B♭ D♭. vii°7/V: F♯ A C E♭. vii°7/vi: G♯ B D F.

The fully diminished belongs to a minor tonic and the half-diminished to a major one, so only the major targets IV and V admit it: viiø7/IV is E G B♭ D, viiø7/V is F♯ A C E. The fully diminished is the more common in both cases.

F♯ A C E♭ to G B D G. F♯ is the leading tone and rises to G. C is the diminished fifth above the root and falls to B. E♭ is the chordal seventh and falls to D. A moves to G. No sensitive tone is doubled.

4, chromatic pre-dominants

chromatic pre-dominants

Spell the Italian, French, and German augmented sixths and the Neapolitan sixth in each key. Mark the doubling where the chord takes one, and put the right note in the bass.

  1. C minor
  2. A minor
  3. D minor

C minor. It+6: A♭ C F♯, C doubled. Fr+6: A♭ C D F♯. Ger+6: A♭ C E♭ F♯. N6: D♭ F A♭, F in the bass and doubled.

A minor. It+6: F A D♯, A doubled. Fr+6: F A B D♯. Ger+6: F A C D♯. N6: B♭ D F, D in the bass and doubled.

D minor. It+6: B♭ D G♯, D doubled. Fr+6: B♭ D E G♯. Ger+6: B♭ D F G♯. N6: E♭ G B♭, G in the bass and doubled.

♭6̂ is in the bass of all three augmented sixths, and the augmented sixth itself is the interval from ♭6̂ up to ♯4̂.

5, resolutions

chromatic pre-dominants

Give the destination of every tone, by scale degree.

  1. The three tones of It+6, including the doubling.
  2. The tone that separates Fr+6 from It+6.
  3. The tone that separates Ger+6 from It+6, and the chord Ger+6 moves through on its way to V.
  4. The four tones of N6, and what happens to the fifth of the V7 that follows.
  1. ♭6̂ in the bass falls to 5̂. ♯4̂ rises to 5̂. 1̂ moves to 7̂, and the doubled 1̂ moves to 2̂.
  2. 2̂, which is held, or moves to the seventh where the dominant arrives as V7.
  3. ♭3̂, which is held into a cadential six-four. Moving Ger+6 straight to V puts parallel fifths between ♭6̂ – ♭3̂ and 5̂ – 2̂.
  4. 4̂ in the bass rises to 5̂. The doubled 4̂ stays where it is and becomes the seventh of V7. ♭2̂ leaps down to 7̂. ♭6̂ falls to 5̂. The V7 arrives without its fifth.

6, function

function and applied chords · master function and prolongation chart

Give the function of each chord in C major.

  1. V/V
  2. vii°7/vi
  3. V7/IV
  4. Ger+6
  5. N6
  6. V/iii
  1. Dominant.
  2. Mediant.
  3. Pre-dominant.
  4. Pre-dominant.
  5. Pre-dominant.
  6. Mediant.

An applied chord takes the function of the chord it points at. A tonicization carrying more than one applied chord is an extended tonicization, and the chords it governs form an embedded phrase model: ii/V and V7/V arriving on V are a pre-dominant and a dominant inside the dominant space.

  1. F A♭ C. ♭6̂.

  3. A♭ C E♭. ♭6̂ and ♭3̂.

Mixture works mostly through pre-dominant function. ♭6̂ is the degree most often involved: it drives by half step to 5̂ and colors every pre-dominant, and it leaves the mode of the passage standing where ♭3̂ would put it in question.
