### criteria for the four-voice writing

The realizations admit many correct solutions. What is marked:

Spacing. No gap over an octave between soprano and alto or alto and tenor. Bass to tenor may exceed an octave.

Leading tone. 7̂ resolves up to 1̂ at every V to I and V to i, and in an outer voice it must. The raised 7̂ in nos. 3 and 6 is written in.

Sevenths. The seventh of V7 resolves down by step.

Parallels. No parallel fifths or octaves between any pair of voices. Direct fifths and octaves into an outer-voice perfect interval by similar motion with a leap in the soprano are marked.

Doubling. Root doubled in root-position triads. In vii°6, ii6 and iv6 the bass is doubled. The leading tone is never doubled.

Range and motion. Each voice stays in its own range, no voice crosses another, and upper voices move by step or small leap where the harmony allows.

Nonchord tones. Where one is written in, it is a passing tone between chord tones a third apart, a neighbor over a repeated chord tone, or a suspension prepared as a chord tone and resolved down by step.
