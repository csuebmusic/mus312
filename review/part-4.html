2, the three stages

the three stages of modulation

Name the stage each event belongs to.

  1. A seventh is added to the tonic triad.
  2. A chord is heard in both keys at once and the syntax shifts under it.
  3. The new tonic is prolonged and cadences a second time.
  4. An authentic cadence lands in the new key.
  5. ♭3̂ is borrowed and the tonic turns minor.
  6. The target acquires a pre-dominant and a cadential six-four of its own.

3, destabilizing the tonic

destabilizing the tonic

Write each of the three in D major, in four voices in keyboard style, with the roman numerals under the bass and the key it opens onto written beside them. Prolong the tonic first, two or three chords of it, then destabilize it.

  1. A prolonged tonic, then a seventh added to it, with the chord that seventh moves to.

  2. A prolonged tonic, then a borrowing from the parallel minor, with the chord it opens onto.

  3. A prolonged tonic, then the same tonic passed through rather than arrived at, over a bass rising by step.
