description

The exploration and analysis of music styles and structural forms of the European art music of the 18th and 19th centuries. Topics include phrase and sentence structures; thematic organization; binary and ternary forms; variation and rondo forms; slow movement forms; and sonata forms.

learning outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course students will be able to:

  • demonstrate understanding of the forms used in tonal and modal music.
  • analyze and evaluate works of music in various forms in their technical, aesthetic, and historical contexts.
  • apply the knowledge learned in earlier theory courses to extended musical works.
  • implement the principles and concepts studied in class via performance on their major instrument or voice.

the coursework

Twelve homework packets carry the term, one for each chapter of Caplin we read. A final analysis closes it.

homework packets

A packet is the end-of-chapter material from the chapter we have just finished: Reviewing the Theory, Examples for Analysis, and, in Part I, Model Composition. All twelve are distributed as one PDF through Canvas at the start of the term.

Hand in the Examples for Analysis, all of them. Annotate each score by Caplin's guidelines: formal analysis above the staff, harmonic and cadential analysis below, horizontal brackets on the exact boundaries of the formal units, and the cadence labels PAC, IAC, and HC boxed at the point of cadential arrival. Break into prose where the annotation alone cannot carry the reading.

Work the review questions on your own and check them against Caplin's companion website, which carries the answers. Mark anything you want me to clarify and bring it to class or to office hours. These are not handed in. Model Composition is not assigned; the pages are in the packet if you want them.

Each packet is due at the start of the first meeting on the next chapter, on paper, in class. The due dates are in the schedule below. The packets are weighted equally, so each is worth just under 6% of the course grade.

final analysis

One movement, analyzed in full, chosen in consultation with me by Monday, November 9. The written analysis is due in the final exam slot on Monday, December 7, and is submitted through Canvas by the start of it. It runs six to eight pages including an annotated score and a formal diagram, and it reaches a claim about how the movement works rather than a list of labels. Every claim is tied to a measure number.

In the slot you perform a passage from the movement on your major instrument or voice, or at the piano, and say what your analysis claims about it. The performance need not be polished. It needs to show the point.

tools for the course

Two interactive tools support the harmony work and stay available all term.

The harmony review covers the chromatic harmony this course assumes and extends: the scale types, intervals, diatonic triads and seventh chords, figured bass, inversions, and nonchord tones; harmonic function, prolongation, cadence, and the diatonic sequences; applied chords, the augmented sixths, the Neapolitan, and modal mixture; and modulation, pivot chords, and closely related keys. Every example on the page can be played.

The chord finder takes up to four notes entered from the bass up and returns the stack of thirds with the distance between each pair, the quality, the chord symbol, the bass note and its figure, and, in a key you set, the roman numeral and the harmonic function.

use of AI

Generative AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, and similar tools) is permitted in this course for one narrow purpose: checking grammar and mechanics in writing you have already drafted. Everything else is off limits. The tools may not identify chords, assign roman numerals or function labels, locate cadences or formal boundaries, produce a formal diagram, or generate analytical prose. The analysis and the writing are yours.

These tools misread notation, mislabel chromatic harmony, and assert formal boundaries that the score does not support. You are responsible for every label in your work.

The harmony review and the chord finder are not generative AI. They compute from what you enter and return the same answer every time, and their use is unrestricted.

Every use of AI, including the permitted one, must be disclosed. Undisclosed use, or use beyond grammar checking, will be treated as a violation of the CSUEB Academic Dishonesty Policy and may be subject to disciplinary action. This is the course's Generative AI use policy under CSUEB policy 25-26 CIC 1. For CSU guidance on the ethical and responsible use of AI, see the CSU AI resource site.

Sonata-form terminology follows Hepokoski and Darcy. Harmonic function follows Laitz. Elsewhere formal terminology follows Caplin.

policies

attendance and participation. Attendance and promptness at every meeting are expected. If an emergency arises, contact me by email as soon as possible. You remain responsible for material and assignments covered in your absence: ask a classmate or come to office hours.

late and make-up work. Late work is accepted. A packet is due at the start of class; a packet handed in later that day is one day late. A late submission is reduced by 2% of its value for each day it is late, with any part of a day counting as a day. The reduction stops at half its value. Late work comes back without written feedback; for feedback, submit on time or bring the work to office hours. Make-up arrangements for a documented emergency are handled case by case. There is no extra credit.

required materials. Every reading and score is required, and all of them are provided. The three texts are Caplin, Analyzing Classical Form; Laitz and Bartlette, The Graduate Review of Tonal Theory; and Hepokoski and Darcy, Elements of Sonata Theory. Materials are distributed through Canvas or placed on library reserve, so there is nothing to purchase. The twelve homework packets come as one PDF through Canvas at the start of the term. Bring a score to every meeting.

technical requirements. The course runs on the campus LMS (Canvas). You need reliable internet and a way to view and mark scores. The course tools run in any current browser with no installation. Minimum technical requirements and campus technical support are in the University Resources loaded into the course shell.
